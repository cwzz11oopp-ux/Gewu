# Paper Plan

Build a plan that can be reviewed by a human before writing.

Return:

- a precise, non-promotional title;
- the paper's research question and terminal verdict;
- two to four contributions supported by artifacts;
- a section list with purpose, key points, evidence IDs, expected length, and planned citations;
- a claims-evidence matrix;
- figures and tables with exact data sources;
- limitations that must remain visible;
- references selected for direct relevance, not keyword similarity.

## Story logic

Use this order:

1. What concrete limitation or uncertainty exists?
2. Why does the selected hypothesis address it?
3. How was the hypothesis made falsifiable?
4. What controlled experiment was run?
5. What changed across iterations and why?
6. What did the final evidence support or falsify?
7. What can and cannot be concluded?

Do not plan a positive-result story when the final verdict is negative. A well-supported negative finding is a valid paper direction.

## Plan gate

Do not draft sections until the human confirms the plan. If the human supplies feedback, revise the plan and present it again.
